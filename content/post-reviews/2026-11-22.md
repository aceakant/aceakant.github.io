## What's working
- **Teasers are the strongest posts.** The Fraud ROI teaser got the most impressions (164) and the most engagement, and both people who clicked read the whole article. The Early-Warning teaser and the launch also did well (81 and 87).
- **The right people are seeing the posts:** senior banking and finance people, in India, the UK, Ireland and Australia.
- The no-link insight post got more impressions than the game post (87 vs 55), so LinkedIn does show posts without links more widely.
- Visits from LinkedIn to the site went up from 2 to 16 a week, and 22 people followed in the week.

## What isn't
- **Two of five weekday posts were lost.** One run was skipped by GitHub. The other was lost because the job posts only once a day and the teaser took the slot.
- **Hardly anyone reacts, and nobody comments.** There were 10 engagements on 593 impressions and 0 comments all week, even though posts end with "tell me below".
- **The weekly article post is weak.** The article itself got 44 impressions, against 164 for its teaser. The earlier article shares were also low (72 and 179, with 0–1 reactions).
- **Only a small share of followers sees each post.** Most posts reach 15–75 people out of 4,783 followers.
- **Games: people stop at the hook.** Scam or Legit got 1 click and no play. The Chart Tricks video was watched for 8 seconds on average.
- The insight post got wider reach but 0 reactions, so its text or picture isn't prompting a response yet.

## Suggested changes (need Sweta's OK)
1. **Never lose a weekday post.** When a teaser and a regular post fall on the same day, post both, a few hours apart. Also add a second daily backup run in case GitHub skips one.
2. **Post the Saturday article like a teaser,** with a picture, a short hook and a tracked link, instead of the plain link card that gets few impressions. Add the ?ln= tag so we can count its readers.
3. **Get the first comments going.** Ajay answers his own post's question in the first comment within an hour of posting, and replies to anyone who reacts. End each post with one easy question that takes a single word to answer (for example "A or B?") rather than "tell me below". LinkedIn shows a post to more people once it gets comments, and this is the cheapest way to lift reach.

Keep everything else the same for week 2. The keep-or-change decision on daily posting comes in the 13 Dec review.

## Decision (22 Nov)
Sweta approved change 2 only. From Sat 28 Nov the article post is short (opening line + closing question), uses a link card with the STAR picture, and carries a tracked ?ln=a-<slug> link. Changes 1 and 3 are not being made for now.
