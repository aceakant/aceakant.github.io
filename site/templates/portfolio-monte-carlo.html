How the simulation works

The book. Each loan gets a product, amount, rate, credit score and chance of default. Scores are scaled the usual way: every 20 points doubles the odds of a customer staying good.

Each simulated year first draws the state of the economy. A weak year raises every loan's chance of default together, more so when defaults "move together" more (the one-factor model behind the Basel capital rules). It then draws which loans default, which applications were fraud that slipped past the controls, and which card accounts were run up and abandoned ("bust-out"). Fraud losses are mostly the whole amount; credit losses are what's owed after recoveries.

Profit = interest and fees on loans that keep paying − funding cost − running costs − credit losses − fraud losses − the cost of fraud checks. Repeating that 5,000 times gives the range of outcomes: the expected year, the chance of a loss and the bad years (the 5th and 1st percentiles).

Dummy data, generated in your browser; not any lender's real portfolio. Real portfolio models also cover prepayment, multi-year terms, recoveries timing, capital and fairness. Built by {{ site.author }}.
