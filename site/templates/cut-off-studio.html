How the sample book works

Each applicant has a score built the way credit scorecards usually are: every 20 points doubles the odds of a customer staying good. The odds at a score of 600 are set for each book below, and applicant scores follow a bell curve. A good loan earns its lifetime margin; a bad one loses what's owed after recoveries.

The most profitable rule is to approve everyone whose chance of going bad is below profit per good ÷ (profit per good + loss per bad). The studio shows that break-even and the cut-off it implies. A downturn makes every score 1.8 times as likely to go bad, which is why the best cut-off moves up.

An illustrative sample, not any lender's real data. Real cut-off decisions also weigh affordability rules, fairness across customer groups, capital and the lender's risk appetite. Built by {{ site.author }}.
