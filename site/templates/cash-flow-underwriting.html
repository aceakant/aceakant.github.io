Who does the credit file miss?

A credit bureau file tells a lender a lot about people with long credit histories and very little about everyone else. With open banking, applicants can share their bank-account cash flow too: steady income, essential spending, the money left at month end. Decide the same 5,000 sample applicants twice, and see how many more you can approve at the same bad rate, and who they are. Runs in your browser; nothing is sent.

How it's worked out

The applicants. 5,000 made-up applicants. Each has a hidden "how well they manage money" value that sets their chance of not repaying (about 8% on average, a little higher for thin files). The bureau file reflects that value well for people with long credit histories, and only faintly for thin files, which also score lower simply for having little history. Bank-account cash flow reflects it about as well for everyone, through four signals: months with steady income, essential spending as a share of income, money left at month end, and days overdrawn. These are combined into a cash-flow score.

The two policies. Bureau only ranks everyone by bureau score. With cash flow ranks applicants who share their bank data on a blend: for long credit files, your chosen weight on cash flow; for thin files, 80% cash flow and 20% bureau, because their bureau file says so little. Applicants who don't share are ranked on the bureau score, as before. Each policy approves from the top of its ranking for as long as the expected bad rate of everyone approved stays within your appetite.

Money. Each good loan earns 14% of 6,000 over its life; each bad loan loses 65% of it after recoveries. Extra profit is the difference between the two policies for one month of 5,000 applications, times 12.

An illustrative sample, not any lender's real data. Real cash-flow underwriting needs the applicant's clear consent, checks the bank data is genuine, tests outcomes for fairness across customer groups, and follows each market's open-banking and credit rules. Built by {{ site.author }}.
