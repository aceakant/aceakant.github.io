How it's worked out

The sample lender. A consumer lender with 4,000 million of loans. Each measure has four quarters of history, then two paths for the next four quarters: the plan, and a downturn in which arrears, losses and complaints rise, borrowers drift into riskier grades, and capital and liquidity fall. Both paths assume 8% lending growth. Faster growth pushes most measures towards their limits, and slower growth eases them: for example, every point of growth above 8% lowers the capital ratio by 0.12 points and adds half a point to lending in the riskiest grades by the end of the year.

Triggers and limits. A trigger is the early-warning line: past it, a measure is amber and the Chief Risk Officer acts. A limit is the line the board has said not to cross: past it, a measure is red. For measures where lower is safer (losses, arrears, concentration, fraud, complaints) the lines are ceilings; for capital, liquidity and the score's power they are floors. The trigger is always kept on the safe side of the limit.

Calibration. A measure is well set when it's green today, the plan doesn't break it, and in a downturn it turns amber at least a quarter before it turns red (or turns amber and the limit holds). Too tight: amber already today, so nobody reads it. Plan breaks it: the growth plan and the appetite disagree. Too loose?: green even in a downturn. No warning: trigger and limit break in the same quarter. Fastest growth is the highest growth, in steps of half a point, at which no switched-on measure turns red over the next four quarters.

The statement. The plain draft is filled in from your settings in your browser. The AI version is written by Claude, by Anthropic, as a maker and a checker; it receives only the measure names, your numbers, their status and your stance. Your browser then checks that each line carries your exact trigger and limit.

An illustrative sample, not any lender's real figures or any regulator's minimums. Real risk appetite frameworks also cover operational, conduct, model and climate risk, are cascaded into business limits, and are approved by the board each year. Built by {{ site.author }}.
